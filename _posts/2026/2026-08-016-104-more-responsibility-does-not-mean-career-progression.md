# 

One of the easiest career mistakes is to confuse **more responsibility with career progression**.

Your manager gives you a bigger project, more PR reviews, additional coordination, or responsibility for another service. It feels like recognition: *“They trust me, so I must be preparing for the next level.”*

Sometimes that's true. Sometimes the organisation simply needs someone reliable to solve today's problems.

## More Responsibility Can Mean Two Things

**Developmental responsibility** increases your:

* scope and influence
* decision-making
* visibility
* strategic exposure
* ownership

Your **career leverage** increases.

**Operational responsibility** increases your:

* workload
* support duties
* coordination
* repetitive tasks

But your authority and career trajectory remain largely unchanged.

> **Development increases your leverage. Operational responsibility increases your load.**

The danger is becoming the person who can always absorb more work without actually progressing.

## What Should You Do?

Don't automatically refuse additional responsibility. Instead, ask:

> **“What is this responsibility helping me develop toward?”**

If it builds skills and evidence for your next role, take it.

If it's important but purely operational, make the trade-off explicit:

> *“I can take this on, but I'm currently working on X and Y. Which should I deprioritise?”*

If it's recurring operational work, challenge the ownership model:

> *“Do I need to own this permanently, or could we rotate, delegate, or automate it?”*

And if the pattern continues, have a career conversation:

> *“I've taken on significantly more operational responsibility recently. I want to make sure I'm also developing toward Tech Lead. What responsibilities should I take on to demonstrate I'm ready for that role?”*

This changes the conversation from **“Will you give me more work?”** to **“How do I build toward the next level?”**

## The Rule

Don't refuse responsibility simply because it is more work.

Instead:

**Take developmental responsibility.
Negotiate unavoidable operational responsibility.
Delegate, rotate, or automate recurring low-value work.**

Most importantly, don't silently perform the next role indefinitely.

> **The mistake isn't taking on more responsibility. The mistake is assuming that more responsibility automatically means you're moving up.**

Your goal is not to become the person who is **most useful in the current role**.

Your goal is to become the person who is **demonstrably ready for the next one**.
